Body-Worn Camera Use Case Playbook

How agencies actually use body-worn cameras day to day, and the adoption patterns a CSM should look for (or push on) at each stage of the customer lifecycle. Share this directly with a customer, or use it to spot adoption gaps.

Core use cases

1

Patrol & routine calls for service

The highest-volume, lowest-friction use case — activation should be near-automatic (see prescriptive triggers below). This is where adoption habits get built or lost.

2

Traffic stops

High scrutiny, high evidentiary value. Agencies often pair this with in-car video (Axon Fleet) for a two-angle record of the stop.

3

Use-of-force incidents

The scenario body cameras were built to address first. Footage here should flow directly into the use-of-force review and documentation workflow — see the Training Catalog's de-escalation/documentation course.

4

Interviews & interrogations

Station-based recording, typically via Axon Interview rather than a body camera. Chain-of-custody and disclosure requirements are stricter here.

5

Evidence collection at the scene

Officers narrating and recording physical evidence, injuries, and scene conditions in real time — reduces reliance on after-the-fact written reports alone.

6

Community engagement & de-escalation

Increasingly used defensively for the officer — footage of a calm, professional interaction is powerful when a complaint is filed.

Prescriptive usage — activation triggers

Agencies with strong adoption typically automate activation rather than relying purely on officer judgment. Common triggers to recommend:

  • Lights and/or siren activation (auto-triggers the paired body camera)
  • Holster removal (TASER or firearm draw)
  • Vehicle door opening during an active call
  • Speed threshold exceeded (pursuit detection)
  • Manual activation remains the fallback for anything not covered above — policy should be explicit about when manual activation is mandatory

Common adoption blockers — and how a CSM helps

BlockerWhat it looks likeCSM response
Unclear policyOfficers unsure when activation is requiredPoint them to a written prescriptive-usage policy; offer the de-escalation/documentation training session
Review workflow frictionSupervisors not actually reviewing footage — it just accumulatesWalk through Evidence.com review queues and category-based routing in an admin refresher

What "good" usage looks like at each stage

  • First 30 days: devices assigned and charging correctly, daily upload happening reliably, no major activation gaps on high-risk call types
  • Day 60–90: activation approaching policy compliance targets, supervisors actively reviewing footage, first use-of-force or complaint case resolved with footage as key evidence
  • Steady state (renewal-ready): activation compliance is a non-issue, footage is routinely cited in case outcomes, and the agency can point to at least one concrete story of the program paying for itself
