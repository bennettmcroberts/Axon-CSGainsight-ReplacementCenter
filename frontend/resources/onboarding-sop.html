New Customer Onboarding — Standard Operating Procedure

The default 90-day onboarding runbook for a new Axon customer, from kickoff through the first value review. Use it as-is or as a starting point when a CSM auto-generates a success plan — every milestone below maps to a milestone in the Success Plans tab.

Purpose & roles

Get a new agency from signed contract to confident day-to-day use of their Axon products, with a clear owner for every step.

RoleResponsible for
CSM (owner)Overall plan, stakeholder relationship, escalation, value tracking
Deployment / ProvisioningEvidence.com org setup, device staging, integrations
TrainingAdmin and end-user training sessions (see the Training Catalog resource)
Agency IT / Admin leadNetwork, SSO, device policy on the customer side
Agency executive sponsorInternal change management, policy sign-off

Timeline

1

Day 0–7 — Executive kickoff & welcome call

Introduce the CSM, confirm the executive sponsor and day-to-day admin contact, restate the goals from the sales cycle, and set expectations for the next 90 days.

2

Day 7–14 — Stakeholder mapping & success criteria

Identify every role that touches the rollout (see the stakeholder template below) and agree on 2–3 measurable success criteria for the 90-day review.

3

Day 14–30 — Deployment & provisioning

Evidence.com org configuration, user roles/permissions, device assignment and staging, category & retention policy setup, redaction license allocation. Use the provisioning checklist below.

4

Day 30–45 — Admin & end-user training

Schedule sessions from the Training Catalog resource — at minimum, Evidence.com Administrator Certification for admins and Body-Worn Camera Field Operations for end users.

5

Day 45–60 — Go-live / first adoption checkpoint

Confirm devices are in daily use, check early usage data for gaps (see the Use Case Playbook resource for what "good" usage looks like), and resolve any early support cases quickly — this is the highest-risk window for stalled adoption.

6

Day 60–90 — 90-day value review / QBR

Present against the success criteria agreed on Day 14. Use the Renewal Value Recap template as a starting structure even though this isn't a renewal yet — it's the same muscle.

Kickoff call agenda (Day 0–7)

  1. Introductions — CSM, admin contact, executive sponsor
  2. Restate the problem being solved and the goals from the sales cycle
  3. Confirm scope: products, quantities, sites/precincts, go-live target date
  4. Walk the 90-day timeline above and confirm dates
  5. Confirm the stakeholder map (below) with names, not just roles
  6. Set the cadence: a recurring 15–30 min check-in through go-live
  7. Send a written recap within 24 hours

Stakeholder mapping template

RoleNameWhy they matter
Executive sponsor—Owns the budget and the internal "why we bought this"
IT / Systems admin—Owns network, SSO/identity, device management policy
Field supervisor(s)—Owns day-to-day compliance and usage on shift
Records / Evidence custodian—Owns retention policy, disclosure & redaction workflow
Training coordinator—Schedules and tracks required certifications
Union / labor rep (if applicable)—Change-management sign-off in many agencies

Provisioning checklist (Day 14–30)

  • Evidence.com organization created, billing & contract terms attached
  • User roles & permission groups configured (admin, supervisor, officer, records/legal read-only)
  • SSO / identity provider connected, if applicable
  • Category & retention policy schedule configured per agency records-retention requirements
  • Redaction licenses allocated to the records/legal team
  • Devices staged, assigned to individual officers or pooled by shift
  • Docking stations / upload infrastructure installed and validated on-site
  • Fleet / Interview / Air integrations configured, if purchased
  • Test upload end-to-end before go-live

If the timeline slips

Onboarding delays are one of the strongest early predictors of a rocky renewal. If a milestone is more than one week late:

  1. Log a CTA (type Onboarding) so it shows up on the account's worklist
  2. Flag the specific blocker — procurement, IT dependency, training availability, staffing — not just "delayed"
  3. If the blocker sits outside the agency (device shipment, licensing, integration bug), open an internal escalation
  4. Re-baseline the plan's remaining milestones rather than letting them silently slip
