Renewals

Renewal Value Recap Deck Template

A slide-by-slide outline for the value conversation that should happen well before a renewal date, not during the negotiation. Pull the numbers straight from the account's 360 page — Lifetime value, Products purchased, and Health breakdown all map directly onto slides below.

Deck outline

1

Cover / relationship summary

Agency name, partnership start date, products deployed, and the one-sentence "why they bought this" from the original sales motion.

2

Usage at a glance

Devices deployed vs. active, videos captured over the period, storage utilization trend. Pull from Products purchased and account activity history.

3

Case outcomes

1–3 concrete stories where footage or evidence materially affected a case — a resolved complaint, a use-of-force review, a suppression hearing where footage was decisive. Specific stories land harder than aggregate stats.

4

Support & reliability

Open vs. resolved cases, resolution time, any RMAs handled smoothly. Pull straight from the account's Customer sentiment card — lead with the resolved-rate number if it's strong.

5

ROI / cost-avoidance

Frame in the agency's own terms: complaints resolved without formal investigation, reduced training/reporting time, avoided litigation exposure. Reference the ROI calculator if the account team has run one.

6

What's next — roadmap & expansion

Any product the agency has expressed interest in but hasn't purchased (this is the "expansion" bucket, not the renewal itself). Keep this separate from the renewal ask — don't let a big expansion pitch complicate a simple renewal.

7

The renewal ask

Term length, pricing, any changes from the current agreement, and a clear next step with a date attached.

Delivery tips

  • Send this 60–90 days before the renewal date, not 30 — leave room for procurement cycles
  • Present to the executive sponsor, not just the day-to-day admin contact — they're the one who has to justify the spend upward
  • If health or sentiment has been rocky, address it head-on in slide 4 rather than skipping it — a recap that ignores known problems reads as tone-deaf
  • Keep the roadmap/expansion slide optional-feeling — it should never read as a precondition for renewing

When to skip the full deck

For small, healthy, low-touch accounts, a short written recap (email or a one-pager) covering slides 2–4 is enough — reserve the full deck for accounts where the renewal ARR or the relationship complexity justifies the prep time.
