RMAs

RMA Process Guide

What qualifies for a Return Merchandise Authorization, what to collect from the customer before filing, and how to keep a device outage from turning into a renewal risk.

When to file an RMA

CategoryExamplesTypical path
DOA (dead on arrival)Device never powers on out of the boxExpedited replacement, no troubleshooting required
Hardware failureCamera won't record, dock won't charge, TASER self-test failsStandard RMA after basic troubleshooting
Physical damageCracked housing, water damage, drop damageWarranty-dependent — check coverage terms first
Battery issues SafetySwelling, won't hold charge, won't seat in dockPriority RMA — battery swelling is a safety issue, escalate same day
Cosmetic / non-functional wear Not eligibleScuffed housing with no functional impactNot eligible for RMA under normal warranty terms
Battery swelling: treat as a same-day safety escalation — pull the unit from service, do not continue charging, and file a priority RMA immediately.

Before you file — information to collect

  • Agency account number and site/precinct
  • Device serial number(s) — usually on the underside of body cams, base of TASER devices, or dock label
  • Purchase order number and approximate purchase/deployment date
  • Clear description of the symptom (not just "it's broken") — when it started, whether it's one unit or many
  • Troubleshooting already attempted: reboot, re-seat in dock, firmware version, tried on a different dock/cable
  • Whether the unit is currently in active field use or already pulled from rotation

Filing steps

1

Confirm eligibility

Check warranty status and the category table above. If it's ambiguous, file anyway and let hardware support make the call — don't gate the customer on your own judgment call.

2

Submit the RMA

Submit through the Axon RMA portal (or on the customer's behalf if they're not self-serve) with the information collected above. You'll receive an RMA number — this is the tracking reference for everything downstream.

3

Ship the defective unit

Package with the RMA number clearly labeled on the outside of the box. Replacement units are typically not shipped until the defective unit is received, unless a loaner/advance-replacement is approved (see SLAs below).

4

Log it in the account record

Add a note against the account (Escalation tab, or a Case Watch entry if it's blocking cases) so anyone opening the account later sees an RMA is in flight — don't let it live only in your inbox.

5

Confirm replacement receipt

Follow up with the customer once the replacement ships and again once it arrives. Close the loop — a silently-resolved RMA still shows up as an open worry in the customer's mind.

SLAs & escalation

  • Standard replacement: processed within the agency's support-tier SLA once the RMA is approved
  • Expedited / loaner: available when the outage is mission-critical (e.g. an agency down a meaningful percentage of active cameras) — request explicitly, it is not automatic
  • Escalate to the CSM directly, not just support, when: the same device fails repeatedly, a batch of units fails around the same time (possible systemic issue), or an RMA has been open longer than the stated SLA with no update
  • Escalate to hardware engineering (via your CSM) when a failure pattern looks systemic rather than one-off — this is worth flagging even if each individual RMA is being handled fine
